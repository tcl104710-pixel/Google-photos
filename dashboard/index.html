What this tells us

Three takeaways for the product team

Ranking is the bigger problem than understanding. Users often type something close enough, but the right photo sits too far down the results.

People search the way they remember. "Blue dress, beach, before the pandemic" mixes object, place and time, and keyword search handles each poorly on its own.

Rephrasing is a hidden cost. Nearly 3 attempts per search means users are doing the system's job.

The core problem: the vocabulary gap

Why users struggle even when they remember

Human memory is episodic and atmospheric. People remember who they were with, the weather, and how they felt (e.g., "raining in London with Sarah").

Traditional search relies on nouns and objects. It expects users to guess the exact tags the AI assigned to the photo (e.g., "umbrella, street, jacket").

This gap forces users to translate their rich memories into dry keywords. When they guess wrong, the photo remains lost.

Limits of this data

Public feedback skews toward frustrated users, so failure rates here are higher than in the whole user base. Treat the numbers as a map of where pain concentrates, not as product-wide metrics. Interviews and MVP testing are used to validate the patterns.
