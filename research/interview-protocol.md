# Interview Protocol
## Phase 0.3 — Task-Based User Interviews

**Project:** AI-Native Google Photos Memory-Based Retrieval
**Version:** 1.0
**Duration:** 45 minutes per session
**Format:** Think-aloud, task-based, semi-structured

---

## Interview Goals

By the end of each session, the interviewer must be able to answer:

1. What types of photos does this user find hard to retrieve?
2. What do they remember about those photos?
3. What do they forget?
4. What search strategies do they use — and in what order?
5. Where exactly does their retrieval journey break down?
6. What workarounds do they try?
7. What does failure feel like to them (emotional state)?

---

## Participant Criteria

### Inclusion Criteria
- Uses Google Photos as primary photo storage
- Has been using Google Photos for 2+ years
- Has a library of at least 1,000 photos
- Has experienced difficulty finding a specific photo at least once
- Willing to share screen during the session

### Exclusion Criteria
- Uses primarily iCloud or another photo app
- Has fewer than 500 photos total
- Works in photography professionally (atypical usage patterns)

### Target Sample
- 6 to 8 participants
- Mix of age groups: at least 2 participants under 30, 2 aged 30-50, 1+ over 50
- Mix of library sizes: small (1k-5k), medium (5k-20k), large (20k+)
- Mix of technical familiarity: 2-3 low, 2-3 medium, 1-2 high

### Recruitment Script (for screening message)

> "We are a graduate research team studying how people find old photos in Google Photos. We are looking for people who have been using Google Photos for at least 2 years and have experienced difficulty finding a specific photo in the past. The study involves a 45-minute video call where you will try to find real photos from your own library while sharing your screen. All data is kept anonymous. Would you be interested?"

## Session Structure

| Segment | Duration | Purpose |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Welcome and consent | 3 min | Set expectations, confirm recording consent |
| Warm-up questions | 7 min | Establish context and participant relationship with photos |
| Task 1: Find a specific hard photo (think-aloud) | 15 min | Core observation: retrieval behaviour under real conditions |
| Debrief and follow-up questions | 12 min | Understand motivations, strategies, workarounds |
| Optional Task 2: A second hard photo | 5 min | If time permits; for additional data |
| Closing | 3 min | Thank participant, explain next steps |

---

## Section 1: Welcome and Consent (3 minutes)

**Say:**
> "Thank you for joining us today. My name is [name] and I am part of a graduate research team studying how people look for old photos. This session will take about 45 minutes. We will ask you to share your screen and look for a real photo from your own Google Photos library — one that you have been trying to find or that you think might be difficult to find. There are no right or wrong answers; we are interested in watching how you search, not in judging your results. Do you have any questions before we start? I will now start the recording — is that still okay with you?"

---

## Section 2: Warm-Up Questions (7 minutes)

> These questions establish context and ease the participant into talking. Do NOT rush them.

**Q1:** Can you tell me a bit about how you use Google Photos? How often do you open it, and what do you usually do there?

**Q2:** How old is your photo library — when is your oldest photo on there?

**Q3:** Have you ever had a moment where you were looking for a specific photo but just could not find it? Can you describe that experience briefly?
- *Probe:* What were you looking for?
- *Probe:* What did you try?
- *Probe:* Did you find it in the end?

**Q4:** How often does this happen to you — would you say it is rare, occasional, or common?

---

## Section 3: Task 1 — Think-Aloud Retrieval (15 minutes)

### Task Setup

**Say:**
> "Now I'd like to ask you to try finding a specific photo from your library. Ideally, pick a photo that you know exists — you took it or you have seen it before — but that you think might be a bit difficult to find. It could be from a trip, an event, a moment with family or friends. Take a moment to think of one."

*Wait for participant to identify a target photo.*

**Say:**
> "Great. As you try to find it, please talk through everything you are thinking out loud — what you remember about the photo, what you are deciding to search for, why you are trying one thing vs. another. If you get stuck or feel like giving up, say that too. I will not help you search — I just want to watch how you do it naturally. Ready to start?"

### Immediate Follow-Up After Task 1

**Q5:** How did that feel?

**Q6:** At what point did you feel most stuck? What was going through your mind there?

**Q7:** If you had NOT found it (or if they did not find it): what would you have done next? What other strategies might you try?

**Q8:** Is there any information you wish Google Photos had used to help you find it?

---

## Section 4: Debrief and Follow-Up Questions (12 minutes)

> These questions probe the broader pattern beyond the single task.

### On Memory and Description

**Q9:** When you are trying to remember where a photo is, what kind of details usually come to mind first?
- *Probe:* Is it more often the people, the place, the date, what was happening?

**Q10:** What kinds of details do you find yourself struggling to remember — things that would help you find a photo but you just cannot recall?

**Q11:** Have you ever tried describing a photo to someone else to help them find it? What kind of words or details did you use?

### On Search Strategy

**Q12:** What is your usual first move when you are trying to find a specific old photo in Google Photos?

**Q13:** If that first strategy does not work, what do you try next? And after that?

**Q14:** Have you used the Ask Photos feature? If yes: did it help? If no: why not?

### On Workarounds

**Q15:** When Google Photos has not been able to help, have you ever found the photo another way? How?

**Q16:** Have you ever given up looking for a photo and just accepted you cannot find it? How did that feel?

### On Gaps and Wishes

**Q17:** If you could describe a photo to an intelligent assistant in your own words — exactly how you would describe it to a friend — what would you say? Can you give me an example using the photo you just looked for?

**Q18:** Is there anything you wish Google Photos could understand about how you remember things that it currently does not?

---

## Section 5: Optional Task 2 (5 minutes)

> Only if time permits and participant is willing.

**Say:**
> "We have a few minutes left. Would you be willing to try finding one more photo — maybe one that you have been meaning to look for but have not been able to find yet?"

*Follow the same think-aloud observation protocol as Task 1.*

---

## Section 6: Closing (3 minutes)

**Say:**
> "That was incredibly helpful — thank you so much. We are using this kind of research to understand the real challenges people face when looking for photos, so that we can build something that actually helps. Is there anything you would like to add that we have not covered? Any other thoughts?"

*Confirm participant email for follow-up questions if needed.*
*Confirm compensation if applicable.*
*Remind them their data is anonymous.*
